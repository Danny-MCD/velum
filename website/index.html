Velum bundles Tor and turns a folder of files - or a site you've already got running - into a working .onion address, in a few clicks, on your own computer. No hosting service. No account. Nothing published anywhere except by you.

How it works

Three steps, all of them on your machine.

01

Point Velum at a folder - or a port

Hand it a folder with an index.html, or the local port something else is already running on.

02

It asks Tor for an address

Velum generates a private key locally and Tor hands back a matching .onion address - yours to keep.

03

Publish, share, it's live

Copy the address, scan the QR code, or export the key so you can stand up the same identity elsewhere.

What's inside

Enough to be useful, not enough to get complicated.

Static folder hosting

A small local server, built in, serves your files - no separate web server to configure.

Point at what you've got

Already running a dev server or an app locally? Give Velum the port and it publishes that instead.

Your address, your key

The private key behind your .onion address stays on your machine. Export it, back it up, move it - it's yours.

QR code & one-click copy

Hand your address to someone across the room or across the world without retyping 56 characters.

Sites remember themselves

Close Velum, reopen it later, and whatever you had published comes back online automatically.

No account, no fees

There's no service behind this to sign up for. It's Tor and a folder, wrapped in a friendlier front door.

Under the hood

For anyone who wants to see the wiring.

Velum talks to Tor over its own control protocol - the same interface Tor Browser and other tools use. Publishing a site is one exchange:

> ADD_ONION NEW:ED25519-V3 Port=80,127.0.0.1:51342
250-ServiceID=7g4k2m9zqjv3rslxpqe2ndf8t6chb1yao5uw0vhi4dxrz9plkq2c
250-PrivateKey=ED25519-V3:mAZ...redacted...F2A=
250 OK

That's the whole trick: Tor generates the address from the key, and whoever holds the key can reproduce the address anywhere. Velum just keeps the process, the key, and the local server it points at, tidy. Source lives in src-tauri/src/tor.rs if you want the exact wire format.

Before you ask

Frequently asked

Do you host anything, or see what I publish?

No. Velum runs entirely on your computer. There's no server operated by this project in the path between your files and the Tor network.

What happens when I close the app?

Tor stops, and every site you had published goes offline until you reopen Velum. Nothing keeps running in the background.

Who's responsible for what gets published?

You are - the same as anyone running any web server. Velum doesn't moderate, scan, or vouch for content it publishes.

Can I move a site to another computer?

Yes. Export the private key from the site's page and reuse it wherever you run Velum (or plain Tor) next - the address stays identical.
